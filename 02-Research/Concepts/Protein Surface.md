
# Protein shape
- Each amino acid has atoms → and atoms are not points, they're spheres since each atom as a van der Waals radius 
- surface is simply the collection of all the outermost atoms that are in contact with the surrounding solvent (usually water in biology, or crystallization buffer in experiments)
- Why SASA matters 
	- Proteins interact with their environment via their surface
	- In crystallization: 
		- Sufaces must be rigid, ordered and compatible with packing against each othter
		- too many floppy, high-entropy resiudes on the surface (like Lys, Glu, Gln) → crystals don't form 
		- flat hydrophobic or charged patches → help proteins stick in a repeatable way →  crystals form
